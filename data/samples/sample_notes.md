## Introduction and First Law (Inertia) (00:00-00:45)

- Newton's Laws of Motion are the three fundamental laws describing how objects move and interact.
- Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia): An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and direction, unless acted upon by an unbalanced force.
- Example: A hockey puck sliding on ice keeps going until friction or a wall stops it.

## Newton's Second Law (F = ma) (00:46-01:20)

- Newton's Second Law: F = ma (force equals mass times acceleration).
- The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass.
- Greater force produces greater acceleration; greater mass produces less acceleration for the same force.
- Example: Pushing a shopping cart -- the harder you push, the faster it accelerates. A heavier cart accelerates more slowly for the same push.
